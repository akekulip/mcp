# Literature sweep for the review panel (2026-08-27)

Produced by the panel's research agents; every source was opened or resolved via arXiv/Semantic Scholar/web. Unread full texts are marked in the one-line summary.

## Adaptive / learned measurement scheduling for detecting a single rare anomaly under a sampling budget: bandit quickest change detection, active/Bayesian probing, adaptive telemetry systems — and what the theory says about when learning beats round-robin coverage (mapped onto MCP's Tier-1 result and H1)

### Sources
- **Bandit Quickest Changepoint Detection (Gopalan, Saligrama, Lakshminarayanan)** (arXiv 2107.10492v3, 2021 (full text read via pdftotext this session)) — arXiv:2107.10492
  Theorem 2: any budgeted sensing policy has delay >= Omega(log(1/alpha) / max_a KL(theta*(a)||theta0(a))); Theorem 3: epsilon-greedy CUSUM attains it up to 1/(1-eps) PLUS a learning term that scales as O(d^5/(eps^4 delta^8)) in the sparse d-stream Gaussian example; uniform sampling pays d x the oracle delay because its per-sample divergence is the average delta^2/d.
- **Approximately Optimal Multi-Stream Quickest Change Detection (Kartzman, Hawkins, Hale)** (arXiv 2601.22561, 2026) — arXiv:2601.22561
  One-stream-at-a-time sampling, one stream changes by an unknown amount: decaying-eps-greedy switching + GLR gives approximate first-order asymptotic optimality (alpha -> 0) without a discretized post-change set or a minimum change magnitude; light-tailed streams.
- **Online Quickest Change Detection for Multiple Gaussian Sequences Using Multi-Armed Bandits (Kartzman, Robinson, Hale)** (ACM SIGMETRICS Performance Evaluation Review, 2025) — DOI:10.1145/3764944.3764956
  Same setting; proves the adaptive rule matches single-stream CUSUM delay to within a constant factor, i.e. the whole K-fold price of round-robin is recoverable asymptotically.
- **Bandit Change-Point Detection for Real-Time Monitoring High-Dimensional Data Under Sampling Control — TSSRP (Zhang, Mei)** (Technometrics 65(1), 2023 (arXiv 2009.11891, 2020)) — DOI:10.1080/00401706.2022.2054861
  Thompson sampling over per-stream posteriors picks which components to observe each step; local Shiryaev-Roberts-Pollak statistics fused by sum-shrinkage raise the global alarm; evaluated by ADD vs ARL curves in simulation, not by theory.
- **A Bayesian Partially Observable Online Change Detection Approach with Thompson Sampling (Guo, Yan, Zhang)** (Technometrics, 2022) — S2:639f2858e90aafe348a90f359229c9354ae38ac6
  Only a subset of variables observable per step; posterior Bayes factor is the detection statistic and is used as the reward of a combinatorial MAB solved by Thompson sampling — the closest published shape to MCP's 'observation-only reward' slow loop.
- **Data-Efficient Quickest Change Detection in Minimax Settings — DE-CuSum (Banerjee, Veeravalli)** (IEEE Trans. Information Theory, 2012 (arXiv 1211.3729)) — arXiv:1211.3729
  ON/OFF observation control driven by the CUSUM statistic itself (skip observations while the statistic is low, sample continuously once it rises): asymptotically optimal as FAR -> 0 at any fixed pre-change observation-cost fraction, and strictly better than fractional (coin-toss) sampling — the principled version of MCP's attention register.
- **Data-Efficient Quickest Outlying Sequence Detection in Sensor Networks (Banerjee, Veeravalli)** (IEEE Trans. Signal Processing, 2015 (arXiv 1411.0183)) — arXiv:1411.0183
  Multi-sensor extension: unknown subset of sensors changes; per-sensor DE-CuSum with constraints on FAR, per-sensor observation cost and sensor-to-fusion communication cost; asymptotically optimal minimax formulations, again beating fractional sampling.
- **Multi-stream Quickest Change Detection: Foundations and Recent Advances (Halme, Koivunen)** (arXiv 2604.18008, 2026 (survey)) — arXiv:2604.18008
  Current survey of multi-stream QCD under sampling/communication constraints and sparsity; catalogues the field's criteria (Lorden WADD, Pollak CADD, Bayesian ADD; ARL-to-false-alarm / PFA; observation cost) and the ML-for-sensor-selection corner.
- **A Bayesian Approach to Network Monitoring for Progressive Failure Localization (Arrigoni, Bartolini, Massini, Trombetti)** (IEEE/ACM Trans. Networking, 2023) — DOI:10.1109/TNET.2022.3200249
  Active Boolean network tomography: greedily selects the next end-to-end path to probe by expected information utility given prior observations, with a sliding window for node states that change; beats classic BNT and sequential group testing — the natural non-learned 'active probing' baseline MCP does not have.
- **SprayCheck: Finding Gray Failures in Adaptive Routing Networks (Krebs, Amir, Landau Feibish, Silberstein)** (arXiv 2605.03702, May 2026) — arXiv:2605.03702
  Passive per-spine count imbalance on prioritized sprayed flows; localizes a 1.5 % single-link drop within one Llama-3-70B iteration and 0.5 % within 5 iterations at 64 spines — the field's headline metric is 'detected within N training iterations at drop rate p', not median TTL at a budget.
- **ChameleMon: Shifting Measurement Attention as Network State Changes (Yang et al.)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2023) — DOI:10.1145/3603269.3604850
  Controller-driven re-allocation of switch memory between packet-loss and heavy-hitter tasks and toward flows of importance (FermatSketch); attention is over tasks/flows at epoch cadence, no learning, no paths.
- **Sonata: Query-Driven Streaming Network Telemetry (Gupta et al.)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2018) — DOI:10.1145/3230543.3230555
  Iterative query refinement: coarse aggregate first, then zoom switch resources onto only the traffic that satisfied the coarse query — the canonical hierarchical 'coarse-to-fine' scheme MCP could adopt (per-spine/per-leaf counters first, per-path only on suspicion).
- **Dynamic Scheduling of Approximate Telemetry Queries — DynATOS (Misa, O'Connor, Durairajan, Rejaie, Willinger)** (USENIX NSDI 2022; DynATOS+ in IEEE/ACM ToN 2024) — S2:3d97994abf13657acf93142d608148603a39b7bc
  Reframes telemetry as a resource scheduler: time-division approximation multiplexes one switch's stages/memory among queries to meet user accuracy/latency targets; optimisation, not learning; no path or failure objective.
- **INT-Label: Lightweight In-Band Network-Wide Telemetry via Distributed Labeling (Song et al.)** (IEEE TPDS 2024 (INFOCOM 2021 version)) — S2:e6e50aba73f76ce030992d2ccba1807d4a1549aa
  Data-plane sampled labeling with a feedback loop that raises labeling frequency when labelled packets are lost; reports coverage (99.72 % at 20 labels/s; 92 % with 60 % loss) — coverage-rate is the metric, no localization.
- **BRIGHT: A Bayesian Reinforcement Learning Framework for Intelligent Traffic Monitoring and Measurement (Gong, Chen, Zhu)** (ICACT 2026) — S2:8ab97a638a0a82fd7b5eb8b6bcb838d183c30fe0
  Gaussian per-flow posteriors + a linear contextual bandit with Thompson sampling choosing monitoring templates and per-slot measurement quotas on an SDN controller — a published 'contextual bandit picks the measurement plan under a budget' system, so MCP's slow loop is not first of its kind.

### Findings
- THE THEORY IS SETTLED AND IT PREDICTS MCP'S TIE. Gopalan et al. (Thm 2) give the delay floor for any budgeted policy: log(1/alpha) / max_a KL_a, where KL_a is the per-sample divergence a sensing action yields. Round-robin/uniform over d streams achieves only the *average* divergence (delta^2/d in the sparse-Gaussian example), so it is d x slower than the oracle — that is the whole prize learning can win. The adaptive scheme recovers it only up to a second, additive 'time to learn which action is informative' term that scales as O(d^5/(eps^4 delta^8)) for a change of size delta. For a 1e-4 loss step (tiny delta) on 1024 uplinks (large d) that learning term is astronomically larger than the oracle term, so the theory says: at the frozen §14 point no online learner should beat coverage. The Tier-1 pilot (results_tier1_cosim_summary.md: MCP 18 vs uniform 15, cusum == uniform exactly) is the predicted outcome, not an implementation failure.
- LEARNING CANNOT SHORTEN TIME-TO-FIRST-EVIDENCE. Every result in this literature (Gopalan Thm 3 decomposition; Kartzman 2025/2026; Zhang-Mei) splits delay into (i) the oracle's post-change accumulation time and (ii) the cost of identifying the informative stream. Before the first informative sample, all policies are equally blind; a fault at a uniformly random element is first hit no sooner, in expectation, by any learned policy than by round-robin (round-robin is minimax for first-hit; random is worse, as the gate shows: random 23 vs uniform 15). The only compressible part of TTL is what the repo already measures as 'TTL from first observable drop' (oracle 0 / uniform 9 / random 14 epochs). H1's 30 % cut must come entirely from that 9.
- THE 9 EPOCHS ARE MOSTLY COVERAGE, NOT ACCUMULATION. At budget 41/1024 uniform sweeps in ~25 epochs; H27 says a burst keeps a link observable for ~5 epochs and only ~128 uplinks are busy in a burst. So the chance the faulty link is inside uniform's window while it carries traffic is ~20 % per burst — the 9-epoch gap is 'waiting for the sweep to reach a busy faulty link', which is Gopalan's d, not delta. The lever the theory offers is to shrink d: sensing actions with zero divergence (idle links) should never be spent budget on. Probing only currently-busy links makes d ~128 and the sweep ~3 epochs. This needs no learning — it needs the load context that mcp_policy.py already computes but then dilutes with explore_floor = 0.25 and LULESH-tuned floor 0.75 (COSIM-RESULTS.md).
- CONDITIONS UNDER WHICH LEARNING PROVABLY HELPS (assembled from Gopalan Thm 2/3, Kartzman 2026, Banerjee-Veeravalli): (a) budget fraction b = B/d is small, so the coverage penalty d/b-ish is large; (b) the per-visit divergence is large enough that one or two visits separate the changed element from the rest (delta^2 per visit comparable to the threshold), otherwise the 1/delta^8, 1/Delta^4 learning term dominates; (c) alpha (false-alarm rate) is small — all optimality is asymptotic in log(1/alpha), so at loose thresholds learned and uniform policies are indistinguishable; (d) the change persists (stationary post-change) long enough for the learner to lock on — traffic-phased observability (H27) violates this because the informative action turns off between bursts; (e) pre-change divergence between elements is ~0 (a common baseline), else the learner chases nuisance heterogeneity — the PREREG background-loss sweep is exactly the axis on which the KL between faulty and healthy shrinks and the learning term explodes.
- THE FAST LOOP HAS A PRINCIPLED NAME: DE-CuSum. Banerjee & Veeravalli's ON/OFF observation control — observe when the CUSUM statistic is above a low threshold, skip when below, alarm above the high threshold — is asymptotically optimal at any fixed pre-change duty cycle and provably beats coin-toss (fractional) sampling, which is exactly what MCP's TCAM gate at P = attn/65536 does today. The PREREG §7.4 rule (bump on exceedance, decay on clean, clamp) is an approximate DE-CuSum on a 16-bit register. Reframing it this way gives the silicon result (6 % -> 100 % sampling on a shaped uplink, tau_fast 97 us) a theorem to stand on and a standard metric pair (ADD vs pre-change duty cycle) instead of an ad-hoc 'attention' story.
- METRICS THE FIELD USES (Halme-Koivunen survey; Zhang-Mei; Banerjee-Veeravalli; Gopalan; SprayCheck): Average Detection Delay (ADD, or Lorden WADD / Pollak CADD) plotted against Average Run Length to false alarm (ARL, or FAR/PFA) as a trade-off *curve*, not a single point; observation cost as pre-change duty cycle (fraction of samples taken) or samples-per-unit-time; probability of correct isolation / identification when localization is required; regret or delay ratio against an oracle that knows the changed stream; for the networking side, 'detected within N training iterations at drop rate p' (SprayCheck), coverage fraction (INT-label), and memory/bandwidth overhead (ChameleMon, DynATOS). MCP's single median TTL at one h per arm (PREREG §3.3) is a point on a curve the field expects to see whole.
- NOVELTY THREATS. (1) 'Bandit + change detection under sampling control' is an established problem class with theory (Gopalan 2021, Kartzman 2025/2026), Bayesian/TS solutions (Zhang-Mei TSSRP 2020/2023, Guo-Yan-Zhang 2022), and even a reward that is the detection statistic itself (Guo et al.'s Bayes-factor reward is MCP's 'observation-only reward' §7.2). (2) A contextual bandit choosing measurement quotas under a budget on a network controller already exists (BRIGHT 2026, LinUCB/TS on Gaussian posteriors). (3) Bayesian active probing for failure localization with expected-information-gain path selection exists (Arrigoni ToN 2023) and is absent from MCP's 14 baselines. None of these is spray-aware or in the data plane, but reviewers from the SIGMETRICS/INFOCOM side will ask why LinUCB and not TSSRP/eps-greedy-GLR with guarantees, and why no EIG-greedy arm.
- NONE OF THE VERIFIED SOURCES ADDRESSES TRAFFIC-PHASED OBSERVABILITY: in every QCD-with-sampling model an action always yields an informative sample; here an action on an idle link yields nothing regardless of the fault. That is a genuine gap and it is the one MCP's data (H27) exposes: the 'informative action' is time-varying and known from load counters before any fault evidence exists. Unverified whether any 2025-26 paper models observability-gated streams; I found none in this sweep.

### Implications for MCP
- Stop treating the Tier-1 tie as a tuning problem. Gopalan's bound says learning's gain is bounded by d/(effective d) after the first informative sample and is paid for with a term ∝ 1/delta^8; at F1 = 1e-4 on 1024 uplinks with 41 probes the learning term dominates. Write this down in PREREG as the predicted regime and choose the operating point (delta, d, alpha, persistence) deliberately: H1 can only pass where per-visit evidence is strong (F1 >= ~1e-3, or many packets per probe visit), the fault persists across bursts, and the false-alarm bar h is high. If the paper cannot move there honestly, H1 should be replaced.
- Add the baseline that the theory says is the real competitor: uniform/round-robin over *currently busy* elements ('traffic-aware coverage', B10'), using only load counters, no learning. This removes idle links from d exactly as Gopalan's information structure prescribes. If MCP does not beat B10' at equal budget, the slow-loop learning claim is dead at this point and the paper should say so; if B10' alone gets near the oracle (likely: sweep ~3 epochs vs 25), that simple deterministic rule is itself a clean, provable contribution for sprayed fabrics.
- Reframe the fast loop as in-switch DE-CuSum (Banerjee-Veeravalli): two thresholds on a per-path statistic, sample-rate ON above the low one, always-on above the high one. Report it with the field's metric pair — ADD vs pre-change duty cycle at fixed ARL — measured on Tofino (the F6 run already has the raw numbers: 6 % duty cycle, tau_fast 97 us, ratio 907). The provable statement 'ADD within x % of always-on at 6 % pre-change sampling' is stronger and simpler than 'attention weights'. Cite arXiv 1411.0183 for the multi-sensor version with communication cost — that is the NIC-evidence channel.
- Replace or complement LinUCB with learners that carry guarantees in this exact problem: (i) Thompson sampling on the Beta posteriors infer.py already maintains, fused with a sum-shrinkage global alarm (TSSRP, essentially free to implement); (ii) decaying-eps-greedy over the CUSUM/GLR statistic (Kartzman 2026). Both give a citable regret/delay story; LinUCB with a hand-built 7-dim context and a shadow-price knapsack does not, and the knapsack prices are inert in the sim by the code's own docstring (mcp_policy.py: 'prices are inert there'). Add an expected-information-gain greedy arm (Arrigoni 2023) as the non-learned active-probing baseline.
- Report the metrics the field will look for: ADD/WADD vs ARL curves per arm (sweep h, not one h per arm), pre-change observation cost per arm, probability of correct isolation, and delay ratio to the oracle; plus SprayCheck's 'detected within N iterations at drop p' so the two papers are directly comparable. Keep censoring-aware TTL but as a secondary view.
- Borrow Sonata-style hierarchical refinement: keep per-spine and per-dst-leaf aggregate loss/latency counters always on (cheap, in SALUs, this is what SprayCheck measures), and spend the probe/mirror budget only inside the aggregate that deviated. Under spraying every flow crosses every spine, so a per-spine count imbalance (SprayCheck) plus per-leaf counters gives a two-level 'coarse then fine' localizer whose sweep cost is spines + leaves, not spines x leaves. That directly attacks the d in the bound and is implementable in the existing 8+3 stages.
- Position the related work honestly: the bandit-QCD line (Gopalan 2021; Zhang-Mei 2020/2023; Guo-Yan-Zhang 2022; Kartzman 2025/2026), BRIGHT 2026, and Arrigoni 2023 must appear in the novelty matrix; MCP's defensible novelty is the observability-gated, data-plane observation control on sprayed fabrics with NIC evidence reflected in-band — not the bandit. A theory-flavoured contribution is available and unclaimed by anyone I could find: extend the bandit-QCD delay bound to streams whose informativeness is gated by a known, time-varying load signal, and show that load-aware coverage attains it.

## State of the art (2022–2026) in localizing gray failures / silent packet loss on packet-sprayed and RDMA datacenter fabrics: signals, metrics, scale, adaptivity — and what it means for MCP

### Sources
- **SprayCheck: Finding Gray Failures in Adaptive Routing Networks (Krebs, Amir, Landau Feibish, Silberstein)** (arXiv 2605.03702, May 2026 (full HTML read)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03702
  Passive: destination-leaf data plane counts packets of one prioritized, sprayed flow per source leaf indexed by (QP, upstream spine) (<2 KB for 32 spines); localizes by intersecting reports from several leaves; 1.5 % single-link loss found in one Llama-3-70B iteration, 0.5 % in 5 iterations (64 spines, NS-3+Astra-SIM); 8-spine/8-leaf Tofino-1 testbed detects ≥0.4 % loss with 500 K pkts/spine; zero added load (prioritized flow +0.2 %, others −0.25 %); adaptivity = round-robin choice of which flow to prioritize (control-plane reset ~1 min), thresholds calibrated offline on ROC; limits stated: lossless fabrics only, 2-level only, cannot see access links, coverage only on paths application traffic uses.
- **OmniPath Ping: Active Network Measurement in the Era of Packet Spraying (Yang, Lv, ... Tong Yang)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2026, DOI 10.1145/3789240.3829105 (abstract via Semantic Scholar; ACM DL returns 403 — full text still unread)) — https://doi.org/10.1145/3789240.3829105
  Active probing under spraying: in-network OmniPath cache records per-hop telemetry to remove path ambiguity, in-network duplication/dedup gives full path coverage with zero redundant probes, topology-aware concurrency control trades cache SRAM vs probe timeliness; claims 10 ms-level service tracing with full path coverage using 181 KB switch SRAM; not adaptive to network state (coverage-driven).
- **FlowPulse: Catching Network Failures in ML Clusters (Krebs, Gavrilenko, Amir, Landau Feibish, Silberstein)** (HotNets 2025 (full PDF read)) — https://conferences.sigcomm.org/hotnets/2025/papers/hotnets25-final37.pdf
  Passive: leaf switches count bytes per spine-ingress port per collective iteration and compare with an analytical/simulated per-port load model (temporal symmetry); 1 % deviation threshold is a perfect classifier for ≥1.5 % link loss (ns-3, 32 leaves × 16 spines, Ring-AllReduce, lossless, RTO 5 µs); localizes by comparing senders on the same port; fails at 0.8 % loss for radix 32; not adaptive (baseline re-taken after transient faults); AllReduce/data-parallel only, AlltoAll left as future work.
- **R-Pingmesh: A Service-Aware RoCE Network Monitoring and Diagnostic System (Liu et al., ByteDance/BUPT)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2024, DOI 10.1145/3651890.3672264 (abstract verified via Semantic Scholar; NOVELTY-MATRIX row 16 read pp. 554–558)) — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3651890.3672264
  Active end-to-end RDMA probes from commodity RNICs measuring network RTT vs host processing delay, distinguishing RNIC drops from in-network drops; deployed on tens of thousands of RNICs >6 months; 85 % of located problems accurate, all 157 switch problems accurate; periodic, 5-tuple rotation, not adaptive; no time-to-localize reported.
- **Hostmesh: Monitor and Diagnose Networks in Rail-optimized RoCE Clusters (Liu et al.)** (APNet 2024, DOI 10.1145/3663408.3663426 (PDF read)) — https://conferences.sigcomm.org/events/apnet2024/papers/HostmeshMonitorandDiagnoseNetworksinRail-optimizedRoCEClusters.pdf
  Controller-free full-mesh probing between RNICs on the same host (rail-optimized), rotating 5-tuples to cover all switches/links, measures one-way latency and one-way drops; 3-month deployment on hundreds of servers, located 8 problem types; periodic, not adaptive; no TTL numbers.
- **RDMA over Ethernet for Distributed AI Training at Meta Scale (Gangidi et al.)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2024 (full PDF read)) — https://engineering.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sigcomm24-final246.pdf
  Fleet operations paper: routing evolved ECMP → path pinning → E-ECMP with QP scaling → centralized TE; monitoring is PFC watchdog (>200 ms pause), buffer >80 % alarms, congestion-drop counters, and periodic ping reachability tools; no gray-failure localization mechanism or TTL numbers; ECMP flow-level, not per-packet spraying.
- **CSIG: Congestion Signaling for Datacenter Transports (Ravi, Dukkipati, ... Vahdat, Google)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2026, DOI 10.1145/3789240.3829179 (abstract via Semantic Scholar)) — https://doi.org/10.1145/3789240.3829179
  Fixed-length L2 header carrying µs-granularity bottleneck metrics (e.g., available bandwidth) on every packet; used for Fast Ramp-Up CC (−20 % median RPC latency in production) and per-transfer bottleneck-tier attribution; validated on five switch generations up to 102.4 Tbps; always-on, not adaptive, congestion not loss.
- **FANcY: FAst in-network GraY failure detection for ISPs (Costa Molero, Vissicchio, Vanbever)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2022, DOI 10.1145/3544216.3544242 (abstract via Semantic Scholar)) — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544216.3544242
  In-network: neighbouring switches compare per-prefix/traffic-slice counters to detect loss of tiny fractions of traffic 'in seconds', implemented on Intel Tofino with fast rerouting; ISP-targeted but the counter-mismatch primitive is fabric-agnostic; not adaptive.
- **Flock: Accurate Network Fault Localization at Scale (Kanev? — authors per arXiv 2305.03348)** (PACMNET vol. 1, June 2023 (arXiv abstract read)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03348
  Probabilistic-graphical-model tomography over end-to-end measurements plus passive telemetry; >10,000× faster inference than prior PGM, 1.19–11× lower error on same telemetry, 1.2–55× after adding passive telemetry; inference layer only, not a measurement policy, not adaptive.
- **LMon: Efficient Data Center Network Monitoring and Troubleshooting Leveraging ECMP Hashing Linearity and Lightweight Probing (Xun et al.)** (IEEE/ACM Transactions on Networking 33, 2025, DOI 10.1109/TON.2025.3570008 (abstract via Semantic Scholar)) — https://doi.org/10.1109/TON.2025.3570008
  Controls probe paths through ECMP hash linearity, localizes with LASSO + hypothesis testing; one-third of Pingmesh's probes at 99 % accuracy / 1 % false negatives — the closest thing in the field to an equal-budget probe comparison; ECMP only, not spraying, not adaptive.
- **ChameleMon: Shifting Measurement Attention as Network State Changes (Yang et al.)** (ACM SIGCOMM 2023 / arXiv 2301.00615 (abstract read)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00615
  Adaptive sketch memory allocation between loss detection and heavy hitters and dynamic choice of which flows to monitor (FermatSketch) driven by healthy/ill network state; fat-tree testbed; adaptive but rule-based, per-flow not per-path, no localization TTL.
- **Holmes: Localizing Irregularities in LLM Training with Mega-scale GPU Clusters (Yao et al.)** (USENIX NSDI 2025 (existence verified via Semantic Scholar; USENIX PDF 403 — details from search snippets, unverified)) — https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi25/presentation/yao
  Host-side: random-forest detection of abnormal NCCL communication operators plus communication-operator graphs and cross-iteration analysis to localize 'silent irregular iterations'; relies only on collective-communication traces; iteration-granularity, not link-level, not a measurement policy.
- **Evolution of Aegis: Fault Diagnosis for AI Model Training Service in Production (Dong et al., Alibaba)** (USENIX NSDI 2025 (existence verified via Semantic Scholar; PDF 403 — content unverified beyond title/snippet)) — https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi25-dong.pdf
  Production fault-diagnosis service for training jobs without customer-code changes; network faults are one class among many; cited here as the fleet-telemetry framing MCP must position against, not as a localization baseline.
- **Flare: Anomaly Diagnostics for Divergent LLM Training in GPU Clusters of Thousand-Plus Scale (Cui et al., SJTU/Ant)** (USENIX NSDI 2026 (abstract via Semantic Scholar)) — https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/technical-sessions
  Full-stack tracing daemon + diagnostic engine focused on performance regressions; 6,000 GPUs, >8 months; NSDI'26 also has EROICA (Alibaba online troubleshooting) — neither localizes links, both show the venue's current appetite is production-scale diagnostic systems, not simulated policies.
- **REPS: Recycled Entropy Packet Spraying for Adaptive Load Balancing and Failure Mitigation** (arXiv 2407.21625 (v3 Feb 2026; EuroSys'26 per NOVELTY-MATRIX row 26)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21625
  Sender-side per-packet load balancer that recycles entropy values of good paths and re-routes away from failed links in <100 µs using ECN/timeouts — the NIC already reacts to a gray link faster than any localization system; localization is not its goal.
- **The Ghost in the Datacenter: Link Flapping, Topology Knowledge Failures, and the FITO Category Mistake (Borrill)** (arXiv 2603.03736, Mar 2026 (abstract read)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03736
  Position/survey collecting production failure rates (Meta 419 interruptions/54 days LLaMA-3; ByteDance 38,236 explicit + 5,948 implicit failures/3 months; Alibaba 0.057 % NIC–ToR link failures/month; ~1 link flap per 48 s at 3 M GPUs) — usable motivation numbers with primary citations to chase; no mechanism.
- **NetBouncer: Active Device and Link Failure Localization in Data Center Networks (Tan et al., Microsoft)** (USENIX NSDI 2019 (lineage; existence verified via Semantic Scholar only)) — https://naizhengtan.github.io/doc/papers/netbouncer19tan.pdf
  IP-in-IP bounced probes over chosen paths + regression-based link/device localization; the tomography lineage that SprayCheck/OPP explicitly say breaks under spraying because the probe path is no longer controllable; pre-2022, cite as lineage only.

### Findings
- Signal taxonomy in 2022–2026 work: (a) passive per-spine/per-port counters at the destination leaf compared against a spraying-symmetry or load-model expectation (SprayCheck, FlowPulse); (b) active RDMA probes with rotated 5-tuples or in-network duplication (R-Pingmesh, Hostmesh, OPP, LMon); (c) in-network neighbour counter matching (FANcY); (d) per-packet in-band tags for congestion, not loss (CSIG); (e) host-side collective traces (Holmes, Flare, Aegis). Nobody combines a switch-side attention loop with a priced controller; nobody reports results 'at equal budget'.
- Metrics the field actually reports: minimum detectable per-link loss rate (SprayCheck 1.5 %→1 iteration, 0.5 %→5 iterations, testbed ≥0.4 %; FlowPulse ≥1.5 % at 1 % threshold, fails at 0.8 % for radix 32); FPR/FNR and ROC per loss rate; iterations-to-detect (not seconds); switch SRAM in KB (SprayCheck <2 KB, OPP 181 KB); added network load (SprayCheck 0, prioritized flow ±0.25 %); tracing latency (OPP '10 ms-level'); production accuracy (R-Pingmesh 85 % / 157 switch problems) and problem classes found (Hostmesh 8 types); probe-count ratio at fixed accuracy (LMon 1/3 of Pingmesh at 99 %). 'Time-to-localize at equal budget', log-rank on censored TTL, and regret vs oracle (PREREG H1/H5) do not appear anywhere in this literature.
- Scale: SprayCheck 8-spine/8-leaf Tofino-1 testbed + NS-3 up to 128 spines; FlowPulse ns-3 32×16; R-Pingmesh tens of thousands of RNICs in production; Hostmesh hundreds of servers; Flare 6,000 GPUs; Meta fleet. MCP's 4-leaf×2-spine single-chip emulation is at the low end but of the same kind as SprayCheck's testbed; the 1024-NIC htsim trace is comparable to the simulation tier.
- Adaptivity is nearly absent: SprayCheck's only online decision is round-robin over which flow to prioritize (control-plane, ~1 min reset); FlowPulse re-baselines after transients; ChameleMon shifts sketch memory by rule; OPP's concurrency control balances SRAM vs timeliness, not fault likelihood. No published system learns where to measure from evidence, which is MCP's axis — but the field has implicitly argued that axis is unnecessary because passive counters cost ~nothing.
- The loss floor of published passive methods is 0.4–1.5 % per link with detection in 1–5 iterations (seconds). MCP's frozen operating point (F1 = 1e-4) is 40–150× below any demonstrated detection floor, on a lossy fabric, on idle links (H27: 896/1024 uplinks carry nothing per epoch). That regime is genuinely unclaimed, but H27 shows it is bounded by traffic observability for every policy — including SprayCheck, which only covers paths application traffic uses.
- Explicitly stated gaps in the closest competitors that MCP could own: SprayCheck cannot handle lossy fabrics, 3-level topologies, weighted spraying, or access links; FlowPulse cannot handle AlltoAll/expert parallelism or non-symmetric traffic; OPP and R-Pingmesh/Hostmesh are periodic and coverage-driven. MoE AlltoAll on a lossy UEC-style fabric with background loss F0 > 0 is exactly the case none of them covers.
- The 'silent loss has no in-band evidence' conclusion (H7 F1 failure) is contradicted by FANcY (SIGCOMM'22, Tofino): comparing per-slice counters between adjacent switches yields in-band loss evidence within seconds for tiny loss fractions. In MCP's single-chip virtual fabric the two ends of every virtual link are on the same pipeline, so ingress/egress count matching per virtual link is implementable in the data plane today.
- Venue climate: NSDI'26 accepted Flare and EROICA (production diagnostic systems at 6k+ GPUs); SIGCOMM'26 accepted OPP and CSIG (hardware-validated primitives). Both venues currently reward either fleet-deployed systems or a crisp hardware primitive with a simple evaluation, not a learned controller whose headline hypothesis is unmet at its own pre-registered point.

### Implications for MCP
- Threat to novelty (highest): SprayCheck already delivers passive, zero-network-load, in-switch localization on Tofino with <2 KB state within one iteration at 1.5 % loss. A 'budget of 4 % of uplinks' is not a currency SprayCheck spends, so 'faster at equal budget' does not land against it. Redefine budget in the resources SprayCheck and OPP actually consume — switch SRAM (KB), prioritized-flow distortion, probe bytes per link — or drop the equal-budget framing for the passive competitors and compare on the field's metric: minimum detectable loss rate vs iterations-to-localize, with FPR/FNR.
- Threat: OPP (SIGCOMM'26) claims 10 ms-level full-path-coverage tracing with 181 KB SRAM; MCP's slow loop is 96–117 ms and the F1 loop is host-bound at 10 ms. Until the OPP full text is obtained (ACM DL from a campus network), B13 remains an abstract-only reimplementation and any comparison is contestable; obtain it before the main block, as PREREG B13's decision rule requires.
- Borrow FANcY's neighbour counter matching to give silent loss in-band evidence: add per-virtual-link ingress/egress packet counters compared in the data plane (both ends live on the same Tofino pipe), feed the mismatch into the existing attention register. This could turn the failed H7-F1 result into a supported one and would be a simple, provable hardware contribution (τ_fast for loss measured the same way as for congestion).
- Borrow SprayCheck's multi-leaf report intersection to remove MCP's known uplink/downlink ambiguity (single source leaf): the second host (Hulk) or a second emulated source leaf is enough to intersect two reports; report localization granularity (link vs path) as SprayCheck does.
- Borrow OPP-style first-hop duplication for the F1 evidence probe: one host probe duplicated across all spines at the leaf exercises every uplink per probe, directly attacking H27's finding that TTL is dominated by 'when the faulty link is first probed'. That is a cheap simulation experiment (htsim already has the epoch scheduler) and a cheap P4 change (multicast group at the source leaf).
- Adopt the field's evaluation shape for the main figure: ROC / FPR-FNR per injected loss rate (0.1 %, 0.5 %, 1 %, 1.5 %) and iterations-to-detect, with SprayCheck and FlowPulse implemented as-published on the lossless 2-level case where they are valid, then extend to lossy 3-level MoE AlltoAll where they are stated not to work. The clean paper claim is 'first to localize sub-1 % gray loss on lossy, sprayed, AlltoAll-dominated fabrics', not 'bandit beats round-robin'.
- Use FlowPulse's iteration clock instead of a fixed 100 ms epoch: the MoE trace is idle outside three bursts (H27), so re-pricing per collective iteration (or per burst) is both cheaper for the bfrt-bound controller and matches how SprayCheck/FlowPulse count.
- Positioning against fleet telemetry (Meta RoCE, R-Pingmesh, Hostmesh, Holmes, Aegis, Flare): these are periodic, host- or trace-side, and report accuracy of located problems rather than TTL. Cite them as the operational context and as the B3/B11 arms; do not claim to beat them — none of them evaluates in the regime MCP does, so a comparison would be judged unfair either way.
- Metrics to add to PREREG reporting so reviewers can compare: switch SRAM in KB per arm, probe bytes per link per second, added collective-completion-time overhead (already H6), per-link loss-rate sensitivity floor, and localization granularity; keep TTL as the headline only where an equal-cost comparison is defensible (B3, B9, B10, B13).
- Motivation numbers with primary sources exist (Meta 419 interruptions in 54 days; ByteDance implicit-failure counts; Alibaba 0.057 % NIC–ToR link failures/month, via arXiv 2603.03736) — chase the primaries (Llama-3 paper, ByteDance SOSP'25 'Robust LLM Training Infrastructure', Alibaba HPN/Stellar) rather than citing the survey.

## Data-plane telemetry primitives 2020–2026 relevant to sprayed AI fabrics (CSIG, UEC trimming, INT/HPCC, NIC-side evidence, vendor adaptive-routing telemetry) — what is productized, what a Tofino-1 single-switch prototype can claim, and how reviewers judge single-switch emulation

### Sources
- **Congestion Signaling (CSIG), draft-ravi-ippm-csig-01** (IETF Internet-Draft (Experimental), Feb 2024 (expired Aug 2024)) — https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ravi-ippm-csig-01.html
  Defines the 4-B compact tag (16-b TPID, 3-b type, 5-b value in 32 buckets, 7-b locator) and 8-B expanded tag (20-b value, 16-b locator); signals min(ABW), min(ABW/C), max(per-hop delay); per-hop compare-and-replace; reflection is an L4+ header separate from the L2 tag.
- **Congestion Signaling (CSIG) — proposal to IEEE 802.1 (Bottorff, new-bottorff-csig-1125-v03)** (IEEE 802.1 contribution, 10 Nov 2025) — https://www.ieee802.org/1/files/public/docs2025/new-bottorff-csig-1125-v03.pdf
  States CSIG is in 'current largescale deployments at Google', is specified in UEC CSIG draft 0.50 (public on GitHub opencomputeproject/OCP-NETUEC-CSIG), that the 8-B tag adds a 'queue depth normalized by port speed' signal, that the tag carries a 'Do not update (Packet Trimmed)' state, and asks 802.1 to standardize the two L2 tags.
- **CSIG: Congestion Signaling for Datacenter Transports** (ACM SIGCOMM 2026 (published 17 Aug 2026)) — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3789240.3829179
  Abstract (resolved via DOI; full text paywalled): fixed-length Ethernet header carrying multi-bit bottleneck signals captured at µs granularity at line rate; Fast Ramp-Up cuts median RPC latency 20 % and unclaimed bandwidth 60 % in production.
- **Ultra Ethernet's Design Principles and Architectural Innovations** (arXiv 2508.08906, Aug 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08906
  UET: packet trimming forwards headers at elevated priority so the receiver requests retransmission immediately; trimming 'cannot detect corruption drops (only congestion drops)'; ECN marked at egress; probe control packets can be sent along specific entropy values to detect tail loss; per-packet EV selection at the source.
- **sFlow blog: Packet trimming** (sFlow.org blog, Sep 2025) — https://blog.sflow.com/2025/09/packet-trimming.html
  Shows trimming shipped in NVIDIA Cumulus Linux 5.14 on Spectrum switches (256-B truncation, DSCP 11, priority 4, TC 1–3), BlueField adapters react to trimmed packets, and trimmed-packet counts are exported via sFlow as a congestion metric.
- **Packet Trimming — Cumulus Linux 5.15 documentation** (NVIDIA docs, 2025/2026) — https://docs.nvidia.com/networking-ethernet-software/cumulus-linux-515/Layer-1-and-Switch-Ports/Quality-of-Service/Packet-Trimming/
  Productized trimming on Spectrum-4 and later: trigger = packet about to be discarded for lack of buffer; truncation 256–1024 B (default 256); global and per-port DSCP remark; global, per-interface and per-TC trimmed-packet counters; not combinable with the WJH buffer-drop monitor.
- **Implementing packet trimming support in hardware** (arXiv 2207.04967, Jul 2022 (Popa, Dumitrescu, Handley, Nikolaidis, Lee, Raiciu)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04967
  P4/TNA approximation of NDP-style trimming on Tofino-1 (ndp.p4 standalone and integrated into switch.p4/SONiC); shows trimming can be closely approximated on an ASIC not designed for it — the Tofino precedent for a drop-triggered header-forwarding primitive.
- **Next-Generation AI Factory Telemetry with NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet** (NVIDIA Technical Blog, 11 Dec 2025) — https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/next-generation-ai-factory-telemetry-with-nvidia-spectrum-x-ethernet/
  Commercial adaptive-routing telemetry: SuperNIC counts packets marked for adaptive routing, switch telemetry reports actual routing decisions, roce_adp_retrans retransmission counters, per-link RoCE balance across spine/leaf links, streamed via gNMI/OpenTelemetry into NetQ/Grafana.
- **What Just Happened (WJH) Telemetry — solution brief** (Mellanox/NVIDIA, 2019) — https://network.nvidia.com/related-docs/solutions/SB_Mellanox_WJH.pdf
  In-ASIC, line-rate, event-triggered telemetry on Spectrum: on every drop the switch implements the drop-reason counter and captures the relevant packet header, streams only issue-relevant data (the commercial 'attention' primitive: evidence emitted on the drop event, not sampled).
- **RDMA over Ethernet for Distributed AI Training at Meta Scale** (ACM SIGCOMM 2024) — https://cs.stanford.edu/~keithw/sigcomm2024/sigcomm24-final246-acmpaginated.pdf
  §6.3.1: fleet telemetry collects RDMA hardware counters from switch, NIC, PCIe and GPU; three NIC counters localize network trouble — Out-of-Sequence packets (caught drops 'not reported anywhere else'), link-flap counters, Local ACK Timeouts — surfaced to users via RoCET; load balancing is ECMP with QP scaling, not per-packet spraying.
- **FlowPulse: Catching Network Failures in ML Clusters** (ACM HotNets 2025) — https://conferences.sigcomm.org/hotnets/2025/papers/hotnets25-final37.pdf
  Silent-fault detection under adaptive per-packet spraying from per-ingress-port byte counts at leaves vs a per-iteration prediction (1 % threshold); NS-3 only (32 leaves × 16 spines, lossless, 5 µs RTO); localizes by comparing senders on the same spine port; no hardware.
- **SprayCheck: Finding Gray Failures in Adaptive Routing Networks** (arXiv 2605.03702, May 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03702
  One Tofino-1 multiplexed into 16 virtual switches over VLANs on four shared physical links + a second Tofino injecting faults, CX-6 DX/DCQCN; authors say the testbed 'serves for simulating the effects of spraying … rather than absolute performance measurements' and validate against NS-3; metric = packets per spine (7k at 1.5 % loss, 60k at 0.5 %) and training iterations to detect.
- **HPCC: High Precision Congestion Control** (ACM SIGCOMM 2019) — S2:382b54869bb92039a7f7c56de67d70fb2421f604
  Always-on per-packet INT (queue, bytes, timestamp per hop) consumed by the NIC for CC on commodity programmable NICs and switches — the fixed-overhead ancestor of every 'INT 1/N' baseline.
- **No Signal to Rule Them All: A Systematic Analysis of In-Network Congestion Signals** (NINeS 2026 (OASIcs), Mar 2026 (McClure, Dukkipati, Ratnasamy, Shenker)) — https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/OASIcs.NINeS.2026.12
  For congestion control, sophisticated INT signals perform within ~3 % of end-to-end signals and no single INT signal dominates — reviewers from this camp will discount 'richer in-network signal' claims unless the objective is localization, not CC.
- **REPS: Recycled Entropy Packet Spraying for Adaptive Load Balancing and Failure Mitigation** (arXiv 2407.21625 (v6), 2024–2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21625
  NIC-side per-packet adaptive spraying with <25 B per-connection state that reroutes away from a failed link in <100 µs — the NIC hides link failures from the fabric faster than any switch-side loop can localize them.
- **OmniPath Ping: Active Network Measurement in the Era of Packet Spraying** (ACM SIGCOMM 2026) — https://doi.org/10.1145/3789240.3829105
  Abstract (DOI resolved; full text paywalled): in-network OmniPath cache + probe duplication/dedup for full path coverage, 10-ms-level service tracing with 181 KB switch SRAM — the uniform-coverage active-probing primitive for sprayed fabrics.

### Findings
- Productized already (verified in vendor/standards documents opened this session): (a) CSIG 4-B/8-B L2 tags with per-hop compare-and-replace, locator metadata and a 'packet trimmed' state — deployed at scale at Google, in UEC CSIG 0.50, proposed to IEEE 802.1 in Nov 2025; (b) packet trimming on NVIDIA Spectrum-4 (Cumulus 5.14/5.15: 256-B default, DSCP remark, per-port/per-TC trimmed counters, sFlow export), with BlueField NICs reacting; (c) drop-event telemetry with header capture in-ASIC (WJH, Spectrum, since 2019); (d) adaptive-routing telemetry on Spectrum-X (AR-marked packet counts on the SuperNIC, routing decisions on the switch, roce_adp_retrans, per-link RoCE balance, gNMI/OpenTelemetry export, Dec 2025); (e) NIC transport counters used for fleet-wide fault localization at Meta (OOS, link flap, Local ACK Timeout, SIGCOMM'24 §6.3.1); (f) UET per-EV probe control packets for tail-loss detection and egress ECN. Broadcom Tomahawk 5/6 'Cognitive Routing' telemetry and TH6 trimming appear in search snippets only; the press releases could not be opened — unverified.
- No productized primitive gives in-band evidence for silent (non-congestion) loss. The UE design-principles paper states trimming 'cannot detect corruption drops (only congestion drops)'; CSIG carries bandwidth/delay/queue signals only; WJH captures drops the ASIC itself decides to make, not losses on a remote link. The repo's H7 result (F6 congestion: tau_fast 97 µs, ratio 907, SUPPORTED; F1 silent loss: host-bound, ratio 8.8, FAILS — p4/reports/h7-timing-*.md, BRIEF §2) is therefore the expected outcome of the primitive set, not a surprise: the fast loop is fast exactly where the field already has a deployed signal (CSIG), and slow exactly where nobody has one.
- The MCP 14-B CSIG-style tag is a re-implementation of a shipped, standardized primitive and cannot be a contribution; worse, on the prototype it is single-pipe by construction (step5-7-silicon-v2.md N2: the source-leaf gate 'never learns about downstream congestion from CSIG'), whereas production CSIG delivers the bottleneck value to the end host via reflection. A reviewer who knows CSIG will read the F6 fast-loop demonstration as 'CSIG compare-and-replace plus a threshold register', which Google runs on '> a dozen switch and NIC ASICs' (NOVELTY-MATRIX row 5).
- NIC-side per-path evidence is commodity and adversarial to localization: Meta's counters, Spectrum-X's roce_adp_retrans/AR counters, MetaRoCE per-path SACK bitmaps (repo row 4), REPS rerouting in <100 µs. The NIC both holds the best per-path loss evidence and routes around the fault before any switch-side loop can localize it. The repo's B11 (aggregating NIC-only) baseline is the right adversary; the hardware NIC arm (Soft-RoCE, RTT + reorder-confounded PSN gaps, no ECN — H20/H21) is far weaker than what is shipped, so any 'switch beats NIC' claim from hardware will not be believed.
- In-switch spray-aware silent-loss detection already exists in the data plane: SprayCheck (arXiv 2605.03702) counts one prioritized flow per spine at the destination leaf and Z-tests the imbalance (<2 KB SRAM for 32 spines), FlowPulse (HotNets'25) compares per-ingress-port bytes against a per-iteration prediction with a 1 % threshold and localizes by comparing senders on the same spine port. Both are counter-based, coordination-free and need no mirrors, INT or tags — i.e., they achieve the silent-loss fast loop in the data plane that MCP's F1 loop does not.
- How the field measures: SprayCheck reports packets-per-spine and training iterations needed to detect at 1.5 % / 0.5 % per-link loss, plus ROC curves over the sensitivity parameter; FlowPulse reports FP/FN vs fault probability at a fixed threshold, per collective iteration; R-Pingmesh reports localization in ~20 s and audit accuracy (PREREG §5.1); OPP reports 10-ms service tracing and 181 KB SRAM; CSIG reports production RPC-latency deltas. Nobody in the closest work reports a wall-clock time-to-localize CDF at 1e-4 loss; the field's operating points are 0.5–1.5 % and the natural unit is 'packets on the path (or iterations) until detection', which is exactly what H27 says dominates MCP's TTL.
- How reviewers judge single-switch emulation (from the two spray-aware Tofino papers that were opened): SprayCheck multiplexes one Tofino-1 into 16 virtual switches over VLANs on four shared physical links, states explicitly the testbed is for 'simulating the effects of spraying … rather than absolute performance measurements', and earns credibility by showing testbed and NS-3 agree, then scaling in NS-3 to 128 spines. FlowPulse (HotNets) got by with NS-3 alone. The accepted pattern is: functional/logic claims from the emulation, quantitative claims from simulation cross-validated against it, no absolute-latency claims from the loopback fabric. MCP's prototype (4 leaf × 2 spine on 4 loopback links, one TM, one host port shared with the collector — BRIEF §2) is smaller than SprayCheck's and makes a strong absolute timing claim (ratio 907), so it will be held to the stricter standard.
- The 907× ratio is dominated by the slow side, and the slow side is an SDE artifact: tau_slow 88.8–117 ms of which ~95 ms is bfrt reads/decoding (slow-loop-silicon.md via BRIEF §2). A reviewer will note that faster runtime control exists (e.g., RBFRT, arXiv 2501.17271, in the arXiv search results — not opened, unverified) and that a production controller polls counters via the ASIC SDK at ms rates; the honest number to report is tau_fast in absolute µs against the physical bound (one pass through the pipeline), with tau_slow shown as a range across controller implementations rather than as a fixed 100× hurdle.
- A drop-triggered in-band evidence primitive on Tofino-1 is feasible and has precedent (NDP trimming approximated on Tofino-1 TNA, arXiv 2207.04967; Tofino TM deflect-on-drop is a commonly cited TM feature — not verified this session). The prototype already emits fault mirrors equal to injected drops (step5-7-silicon-v2.md (c')); those are WJH-style 'attention on the event', and they are the one piece of the data plane that can see the ASIC's own drops at µs latency — but they see only local drops, never a remote silent link.

### Implications for MCP
- Threat to novelty (primitives): every actuator MCP gates — CSIG-style tags, truncated mirrors, drop-event evidence, NIC per-path counters, per-EV probes — is shipped or standardized (CSIG at Google/UEC/IEEE, trimming on Spectrum-4, WJH, Spectrum-X AR telemetry, Meta counters, UET probes). The paper must say so in one paragraph and stake nothing on the primitives; the only defensible claims are (i) the allocation policy (which path gets the budget, when) and (ii) NIC-to-switch fusion. Both are currently unproven (H1 not met; A7-style fast loop not shown to matter for silent loss).
- Threat to the fast-loop story: for congestion the loop reproduces what CSIG + a threshold already gives production hosts, and the prototype's CSIG path is single-pipe (N2), so the source-leaf gate cannot see downstream congestion — the opposite of the deployed system. Either fix the pipe-locality issue (reflect the CSIG value back to the source-leaf pipe, as CSIG reflection does) or restrict the F6 claim to 'the gate reacts at the hop that observes the exceedance', and stop calling it path localization.
- Borrow (highest value, simple): put SprayCheck/FlowPulse-style per-(dst-leaf, spine) count-imbalance detection in the SALU as the silent-loss evidence source for the attention register. That turns the F1 fast loop from host-bound (10.1 ms, FAILS) into data-plane (per packet), gives H7 a chance on F1, and is exactly the in-band signal the field lacks for silent loss. Cite both as the source of the statistic; MCP's addition is that the imbalance drives a budgeted attention weight instead of a threshold alarm.
- Borrow (metrics): report detection cost the way the field does — packets on the faulty path (or collective iterations) until localization at per-link loss p in {1.5 %, 0.5 %, 1e-3, 1e-4}, alongside TTL — and define TTL from first observable drop (H27 already proposes this). At 1e-4 the 1/p sample bound (~10^4 packets on the path) makes every budgeted policy coverage-bound; showing the SprayCheck operating points (0.5–1.5 %) is where a budget-allocation policy can visibly differ, and it makes the paper comparable to the closest prior work.
- Borrow (actuator model): treat UET per-EV probe control packets and Spectrum-4 trimming/ECN as the UET-native actuators, and frame MCP as 'the policy that decides which EVs to probe and which paths to tag under a bandwidth budget', with OPP-style duplication and R-Pingmesh-style uniform probing as the fixed-policy arms. This keeps MCP deployable without P4 (the switch half becomes a CSIG/trimming consumer, not a new tag) and answers the 'you need Tofino' objection.
- Evaluation posture for a single-switch prototype (what SprayCheck did and reviewers accepted): emulate the largest topology that fits, state that the testbed measures logic not absolute performance, cross-validate the testbed against htsim at the same small scale, then scale in htsim. Report tau_fast as an absolute µs number against the pipeline bound; report tau_slow as a range across controller implementations (bfrt polling, batched reads, a faster runtime) rather than as the 100× pass/fail hurdle, which currently rests on ~95 ms of bfrt decoding.
- Position against the Google/Berkeley skepticism (No Signal to Rule Them All: INT within ~3 % of E2E for CC): do not argue that richer in-network signals help congestion control; argue only that budgeted in-network attention shortens localization of faults that NIC-side load balancing (REPS <100 µs, MetaRoCE) hides from operators. The measurable quantity is 'fault hidden but localized within N iterations at budget B' — a claim the NIC-only aggregating baseline (B11 with real counters: OOS, LAT, per-path SACK) must be allowed to contest with the full commodity evidence, not the Soft-RoCE subset.
- Related-work hygiene: add WJH (drop-event telemetry, 2019), Cumulus 5.15 packet trimming, the Spectrum-X telemetry blog (Dec 2025), the IEEE 802.1 CSIG contribution (Nov 2025) and the Tofino trimming paper (2022) to the novelty matrix; they are the sources a SIGCOMM reviewer from NVIDIA, Google or Broadcom will name. Broadcom Tomahawk 5/6 telemetry claims should not be cited until the press releases or datasheets are actually opened.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We measured what a two-byte per-directed-link witness buys, and costs, on a packet-sprayed fabric against the two most recent passive detectors built for such fabrics, replayed from their own papers over one shared stream, and we measured the witness itself on an Intel Tofino switch. The witness detects a grayhole at a flat median of 22 million packets from 1.5 percent loss down to $10^{-4}$ with no false positive, where the passive cost grows to 114 million packets at $10^{-3}$ and exceeds a 160-million-packet budget at $10^{-4}$. It names the exact directed link where the passive detectors return the uplink and downlink pair, it recovers injected loss exactly on silicon down to six packets in sixty thousand, and it costs about 0.14 percent of a 1400-byte payload plus one pipeline stage. The advantage widens with several independent faults and fails under a fleet-wide shift in loss, where a relative passive test is the better tool, so every claim is stated for independent faults on a stationary fabric. The witness is an existing primitive; the contribution is the measurement and its boundary. In future work, we intend to design and evaluate a corroboration gate that keeps the ledger's exact localization under a common-mode shift.

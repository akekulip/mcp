\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Common-mode load.} The failure of Section~\ref{sec:robustness} is the ledger's largest limitation, and it is a property of the decision rule rather than of the witness. The registers still recover each link's loss exactly under a fleet-wide shift; what fails is the comparison against a floor that moves with the fault. A fix may need a guard that detects when the floor itself is drifting, or a corroboration gate that requires a link's ratio to stand out relative to its neighbours as well as relative to the floor, i.e., the relative normalization that lets SprayCheck hold in that regime. We leave that gate to future work, and we note that it is design work with its own evaluation, not a parameter change.

\textbf{Restoration.} The paper stops at naming a directed link. Systems that then quarantine the link and re-admit it, such as CorrOpt~\cite{corropt}, Aegis~\cite{aegis}, and REPS~\cite{reps}, re-admit on evidence from real or host-generated traffic, and a link that spraying has starved of traffic can supply no such evidence. The ledger's vantage exposes that gap but this paper does not close it; we cite the repair systems to bound what the measurement enables.

\textbf{What the localization result is.} The ledger's exact-localization score of 1.00 is close to definitional, and we say so where we report it. Given each directed link's own transmit count, a detector that flags a link has already localized it. The measured fact is that the passive arms, which lack that count, alias the uplink and downlink pair at low loss and cannot recover it, and that the aliasing is not a quirk of one baseline but a limit of a single receive-only vantage under spraying. The result should be read as the witness removing an information-structure limit, not as an inference advance.

\textbf{The wire cost is a real trade.} Two bytes per packet on every fabric hop is a cost the passive detectors never pay, and the halving from four bytes consumed the program's last ingress stage. An operator who weights pipeline headroom over wire bytes can prefer the four-byte version, and an operator who cannot tag packets at all cannot use the witness. We report both sides so that the trade can be made rather than assumed.

\textbf{An open anomaly on unmeasured sublinks.} During a 57-cycle soak of the two-byte program on the switch, the injected-loss recovery passed in every cycle, but two sublinks that were neither armed nor measured each showed one stamped packet with no matching arrival during clean traffic, a signature not seen across about 3\,200 historical cycles of the four-byte program. Port-level media-access counters ruled out a physical-layer drop, and a fully idle window ruled out ambient noise; the cause is not known. Every number in this paper comes from armed, measured sublinks with baseline and post-traffic reads, and none is affected, but we record the anomaly rather than omit it.

\textbf{Generality.} The replay fabric has four leaves and eight spines with uniform spray, and the silicon fabric is a four-leaf, two-spine virtual topology on one switch. FlowPulse's dilution grows with senders per port and SprayCheck's intersection improves with leaves, so the crossover rates are properties of this fabric. The $1/p^2$ wall for a count-based test should not depend on the topology, since it follows from the spray noise alone. SprayCheck's sensitivity was calibrated under its paper's Poisson model; its real spraying has less noise, which would lower its curve by a constant factor. The budget of 160 million packets sets where the passive action rate collapses, not whether it does.
